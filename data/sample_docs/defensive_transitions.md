# Defensive Transitions

## Definition

A defensive transition is the short period immediately after a team loses possession.
During this moment the team is often out of its defensive shape, because players were
positioned to attack: full-backs may be high, midfielders may be ahead of the ball and
the distances between players may be large.

## Two main reactions

1. **Immediate pressure on the ball (counterpressing).** Nearby players try to win the
   ball back within a few seconds, before the opponent can organise a counter-attack.
2. **Recovery and delay.** Players run back towards their own goal, protect the central
   area and delay the opponent until enough teammates are behind the ball.

Many teams combine both: the players closest to the ball press, while the players further
away recover and cover the most dangerous spaces.

## Rest defence

Rest defence describes the players who stay behind the ball while the team attacks. Their
positions decide how well the team can react to a loss. A typical goal is to keep enough
players to control the opponent's forwards and to block the central passing lanes.

## Tactical fouls and discipline

In some situations a player may stop a counter-attack with a foul. This is part of the
game but carries a disciplinary risk, and it is not a substitute for good rest-defence
organisation.

## Analysis note

Event data can show where possession was lost and what happened in the following
actions. To judge whether players were in good rest-defence positions at the moment of the
loss, an analyst normally needs tracking data or video.
