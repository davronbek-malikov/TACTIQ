LOW BLOCK


What a low block is
A low block is a defensive organisation in which a team defends close to its own goal,
usually with its last line near the edge of its own penalty area.    The team gives up
territory in exchange for compact spaces:   the opponent has the ball, but little room
between and behind the defensive lines.



Typical shape
Common low-block shapes are 4-4-2, 4-5-1 and 5-4-1.    The distances between the lines
are kept short so that a pass into the space between the lines can be pressed at once.
Wide players often help their full-backs, creating two-versus-one situations near the
touchline.


Strengths
- Little space behind the defence, so runs in behind are less dangerous.
- The central area in front of goal is crowded.
- After winning the ball, there may be large spaces for a counter-attack.


Weaknesses
- The opponent can keep possession for long periods.
- Crosses, second balls and shots from outside the box become important risks.
- Counter-attacks start far from the opponent's goal.


Analysis note
Event data can show where the opponent's shots and crosses came from.    To judge how
compact the block actually was, line distances and player positions are needed,
which normally requires tracking data or video.
